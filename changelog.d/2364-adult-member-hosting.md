- **Your club can now ask that an adult member stays alongside non-member guests,
  without ever leaving a member stuck (#2364).** A new **Adult Member Hosting**
  card under Booking Policies lets you say what should happen when a non-member
  guest is booked on a night with no adult member on the same booking: allow it,
  or send that booking to an admin to look at. Clubs with more than one lodge can
  set it once for the club and let each lodge follow that, or decide for itself.

  Choosing "send it to an admin" does not stop anybody. The booking is still
  made and can still be paid — nobody is turned away and nobody has to ring the
  club — it simply appears for you to review. As with minimum-stay rules, you
  also choose upfront whether a future exception request should hold the beds
  while you decide; the choice is stored, carried by configuration transfer and
  shown on the card, and the request-and-approve screens follow separately.

  **Who counts as the adult member.** They have to be on the booking as a guest
  in their own right, on that night. Being the person who made the booking is
  not enough — plenty of members book for family who are travelling without
  them — and child or youth members do not count, nor does a member whose
  membership is inactive, cancelled or archived. Members' own nights never need
  covering; only non-member guest-nights do. A guest staying only some of the
  nights is judged night by night, so a member who is there for part of the stay
  covers exactly the nights they are actually there.

  **The review clears itself.** Add an adult member to the booking, remove the
  guest, move the nights, reinstate a member, or turn the policy off, and the
  flag disappears on its own with no action from you. A review you have already
  decided is only raised again if the problem genuinely changes — different
  guests or different nights — not because somebody corrected a spelling. A
  booking waiting for hosting review is not stopped at the door on arrival: the
  fix is an adult member joining the booking, which is not something anyone at
  the lodge can do.

  **Booking on behalf of a member** is the one place the rule refuses anything,
  and only once: if the party would trip it you are asked for a reason, and that
  reason and your name are stored with the booking, so "who let this through, and
  why" has an answer months later. Nothing is waved through silently.

  When the requirement is switched on, your public booking-rules page states it
  in one plain sentence. It says nothing about asking for an exception, because
  there is nowhere to ask yet.

  **Configuration bundles now need to be re-exported.** The hosting setting
  travels with your other booking policies, which means bundles moved to
  **format version 4**. Older bundles are refused with a message telling you to
  re-export from the current app — the alternative would be an older copy of the
  app quietly ignoring the new setting while telling you it had replaced all your
  booking policies. Re-exporting takes one click.
