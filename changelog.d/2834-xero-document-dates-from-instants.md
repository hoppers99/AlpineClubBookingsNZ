- **Invoices, credit notes and payments raised in the morning are no longer
  dated to the day before (#2834).** New Zealand runs 12 to 13 hours ahead of
  UTC, so for roughly the first half of every local day the club's date and the
  world clock's date disagree. Twenty dates the club sends to Xero were reading
  the world clock's: the date on a joining-fee invoice, a membership
  subscription invoice, a supplementary invoice for a booking change, a
  combined group-settlement invoice, every kind of credit note (refund, account
  credit, booking-modification and membership-cancellation), every credit-note
  allocation, and every Stripe payment and refund recorded against them. A
  credit note raised at 9am on 1 July was dated 30 June.

  All of them now use the club's own calendar — the same one already used for a
  lodge night. Nothing about the amounts, the accounts they post to, or how any
  document is produced changed. Where a due date is "thirty days later", those
  are now thirty calendar days on the club's calendar, which is also correct
  across the March and September daylight-saving changes.

  **Please read this before you file your next GST return.** A document's date
  is what puts it in a GST period and a financial year, so a document raised in
  the New Zealand morning now falls one day later than the same document would
  have before this release. Across a month end — and especially across 1 April
  — that moves it into the following period. This is the correction rather than
  a change of policy: the club's morning was previously being reported under the
  previous day. It is worth knowing about if you are comparing a return prepared
  before this release with the figures after it, and it is worth expecting a
  short run of documents around the changeover that sit either side of a period
  boundary from where you might have looked for them.

  **Documents already in Xero are left exactly as they are.** This changes newly
  raised documents only. Nothing is written back to the accounting system, no
  historical figures are restated, and no invoice, credit note or payment that
  has already been issued has its date moved.

  One small thing gets *better* as a side effect. If your Xero organisation has
  a lock date on a closed period, a document raised in the New Zealand morning
  was previously dated a day earlier and could fall on the wrong side of that
  lock and be rejected. Dating it on the club's day makes that less likely, not
  more.
