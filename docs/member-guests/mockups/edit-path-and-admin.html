Adding a member guest while editing — and the two new emails

MG1–MG3 are merged. This is the small pack: only the things MG4 puts on a screen that nobody has seen before. Everything already signed off is drawn here as settled and is not re-asked.

Eight panels. Six of them are the same section in two places — a member editing their own booking, and an admin doing it for them. Two are emails. Every name, date and price is invented; every word of copy is the real proposed wording.

Before you startWhat is already settled, and not being re-asked

Reused unchanged from MG1–MG3

  • The find box is MG3's, not a new one. One field that takes either an email address or a name, the same hint, the same neutral refusals, the same rate limits and audit. Nothing about finding a member changes in MG4.
  • The badges are MG2's, from the one badge function with three audiences. “Added by the club” is already the shipped label for an admin-assigned row — MG4 does not invent it.
  • An admin add is consent-free and confirmed immediately, and the person is always told (your MG4-D-a tick). The same rule covers a booking copy and the booking-request pipeline (MG4-D-b).
  • An admin searching for a member can search names, always, including under-18s — the club's own open-search and minors switches govern what members may browse, not what the club's own officers may (D-20 / O-4).
  • Self-removal keeps its ordinary limits (D-14), and where the limits bite, the email must name the real remedy rather than a dead end (D-15).
  • Module off means the surface is absent, not hidden.

So what am I actually asking?

Only whether these look and read right. There are four questions at the bottom — on which set of consent words the edit panel should use, on whether the admin notice becomes its own editable email or stays inside the one that already exists, on whether the withdrawal email names who withdrew, and on whether the admin's find box says out loud that it can see more than a member's can. Each has a recommended answer, so “go with the recommendations” is a complete reply.

One matters more than the rest: panel 7. It is the only email a member gets about a booking they never agreed to be on, and one of its sentences has to tell some of them that they cannot take themselves off.

Section CThe two new emails

MG2 shipped five. These are the last two of the epic. Both go to the person being added or un-added — the one who did not initiate anything and has the least idea why an email has arrived.

7 member-guest-added-by-admin — “you are on a booking, and here is what that means”

Where: to the member (or their family delegate) whenever the club, a booking copy, or the booking-request pipeline puts them on a booking without asking

This is the only email in the epic that lands on somebody who was never given a choice, so it has to answer four questions in order: which booking, whose booking, which nights, and how do I get off it. The fourth is the one that can go wrong — for a hand-priced or pipeline booking there is no self-removal link, and offering one that the server would refuse is worse than saying nothing. So the last sentence is computed from the same rule the booking page runs, and the two variants below are the two answers it gives.

Variant A — an ordinary booking, self-removal available

You have been added to a lodge booking

What this panel pins down: that the removal sentence is never boilerplate. Variant B's wording is the honest one your D-15 decision requires — it names both things the club can actually do, rather than the dead-end “ask the club” or a self-removal link that would refuse in the member's face. Variant A names self-removal with its two real conditions, so nobody discovers them for the first time at the moment they want out.

The person-nights sentence is new copy, and it is the plainest true version of a real rule. A member may be on only one live booking per night. Being added consumes that, silently, for somebody who did not ask to be added — so it is stated rather than left to surface as a refusal the next time they try to book something.

One thing to decide about where this lives. MG2 deliberately shipped one template, member-guest-added, covering notify-only clubs, admin adds and pipeline rows, told apart by one composed sentence — so an admin has one place to edit rather than three near-copies. This page draws the email under MG4's own name because that is how the issue described it, but the content above fits inside the existing template with one new token. Whether MG4 adds a second key or extends the first is question 2 below.

8 member-guest-request-withdrawn — “never mind, and that is all”

Where: to the member who was asked, when the booker or an admin cancels a request they have not answered

The shortest email in the epic and the one easiest to get wrong. Somebody was asked a question, thought about it, and the question has been taken away. There is nothing to do, nothing to answer, and nothing to feel bad about — so the ordinary removed-member copy carries no apology, no reason, no “unfortunately”, and no self-service action button, exactly like MG2's lapse notice. A recipient who independently retains bookings-view authority may receive the canonical booking-detail action; an ordinary removed member or family delegate does not. The one sentence doing real work is the third: it tells them the link in the earlier email is dead before they click it and meet an error page.

To priya.kaur@example.nz · Subject The request to add you to a lodge booking has been withdrawn

That request has been withdrawn

Hi Priya, the request to add you to a booking at Silverpeak Lodge on 8–10 Aug 2026 has been withdrawn, and the bed that was held for you has been released.

There is nothing for you to do. You were not recorded as saying yes or no — the question was simply taken back.

The link in the earlier email no longer works. If plans change, you can be added to a booking again later.

What this panel pins down: the neutrality, and the absence of a button. There is no page for this person to visit — they are not on the booking, and sending them to one would either leak it or refuse them.

It does not say who withdrew it. MG2's lapse notice names the booker, because it ends by suggesting the reader ask that person again. Here the withdrawer might be the booker or might be a club officer, and naming either invites a reading the copy is trying to avoid — that somebody changed their mind about you. That is question 3 below; the neutral version is drawn.

Sent only where a request actually went out, on the same rule as MG2's lapse notice: nobody is told a request was withdrawn that they never received.
